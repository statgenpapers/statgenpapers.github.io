StatGenPapers

About StatGenPapers

Each month, StatGenPapers lists the statistical genetics papers that a model predicts readers will find of interest, out of about 2,000 new papers in journals and preprints. Anyone can add a paper the model missed; it shows a person icon. The code is in the public repository.

Where do the papers come from?

  1. Journals (through OpenAlex): Nature, Nature Genetics, Nature Communications, Nature Reviews Genetics, Nature Medicine, Nature Human Behaviour, Science, Science Translational Medicine, Cell, Cell Genomics, American Journal of Human Genetics. No corrections, editorials or news.
  2. Preprints: bioRxiv genetics and genomics; medRxiv genetic and genomic medicine and health informatics. A preprint published in the same month shows as the published paper.
  3. A paper is listed once; the journal version of an earlier preprint can be listed again.

A month's list is built on the 10th of the next month.

What's the score?

The model's estimate of the chance that a paper is of interest; every paper at 30% or more is listed. Tested on 8 past months (March to October 2026), each scored by a model trained only on the months before it:

  1. Papers at about 50% were of interest about half the time.
  2. A paper of interest outranks one that is not 96% of the time (AUC); the top 25 hold 90 of the 210 papers of interest. About one paper of interest in ten comes from a source that is not scanned; adding papers fills that gap.
  3. It comes close to Claude Haiku grading each paper: 38 against 43 papers of interest in the top 25 (August to October), at no cost.
  4. The 30% cutoff listed 14 to 36 papers a month and caught 107 of 258 papers of interest; a fixed 24 a month caught 95.

How does the score work?

  1. The title and abstract become a vector of 512 numbers from a small embedding model, potion-base-32M.
  2. A logistic regression on the vector and the journal gives a raw score, calibrated into the Score.
  3. The model is refitted before each list on the candidates of the 12 months before it: papers of interest are positives, the rest negatives, and a month's weight halves every 6 months.
  4. Interest (▲) re-orders the list at once but does not change the Score; the next month's model learns from it.
