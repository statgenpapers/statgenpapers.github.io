StatGen Papers

About StatGen Papers

Each month, StatGen Papers lists the statistical genetics papers that a model rates likely to interest a lab: every paper with a Score of at least 25%. The model chooses them from about 2,000 candidates in journals such as Nature Genetics, the American Journal of Human Genetics and Cell Genomics, and in preprints. Anyone can add papers the model missed, including to earlier months. The code is in the public repository.

Where do the papers come from?

  1. Journals (through OpenAlex): Nature, Nature Genetics, Nature Communications, Nature Reviews Genetics, Nature Medicine, Nature Human Behaviour, Science, Science Translational Medicine, Cell, Cell Genomics, American Journal of Human Genetics. Corrections, editorials and news items are left out.
  2. Preprints: bioRxiv genetics and genomics; medRxiv genetic and genomic medicine and health informatics. A preprint whose published version appears in the same month is left out in favor of the published one.
  3. A paper listed in an earlier month is not listed again; the journal version of an earlier preprint can be.

Each month's list is built on the 3rd of the following month.

How does the score work?

  1. The title and abstract become a vector of 512 numbers, from a small text-embedding model (potion-base-32M) that runs on an ordinary CPU.
  2. A logistic regression on that vector and the journal or preprint category gives a raw score. It was fitted to a year of a statistical genetics lab's monthly reading list: the papers the lab picked are the positives, all other candidates the negatives.
  3. The raw score is calibrated into a probability, the Score. Every paper at 25% or more is listed, so a month with more promising papers has a longer list.
  4. Readers' marks re-order the list. Once 30 listed papers have marks, they also fit a small monthly correction to the model.

What is the score?

The Score beside each paper is the chance that the lab would pick it for its reading list. In the test below, papers scored about 50% were picked about half the time. Marks re-order the list but do not change the score.

Does the score work?

Each of 8 past months (March to October 2026) was scored by a model trained only on the months before it.

  1. A picked paper outranks one that was not picked 96% of the time (AUC), and the top 25 hold 90 of the 210 picks: fewer than half. Tastes differ from month to month, and about one pick in ten comes from a source that is not scanned. Adding papers fills that gap.
  2. It comes close to a language model (Claude Haiku) that read and graded each paper: on the same papers in August to October, 38 against 43 picks in the top 25, at no cost.
  3. From 2026/01 to 2026/10, the 25% cutoff listed 15 to 39 papers a month and caught 111 of the 242 picks, as many as a fixed 25 a month.

Why do the months before 2026/11 already have marks?

The lists for 2026/01 to 2026/10 were made as if the site had run then: each month by a model trained only on the months before it. The lab's actual picks for each month are shown as marks: a listed pick has one, and a pick the model missed was added ("added by the lab"). From 2026/11 the lists are made live, and the weights are refitted each month as the lab's picks come in.

How do I give feedback?

Open an issue on GitHub: a bug, a journal or preprint category to add, or anything else.
