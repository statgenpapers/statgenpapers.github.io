What this is

Each month this site lists about 25 new statistical genetics papers, chosen by a small model from roughly 2,000 candidates. Anyone can upvote papers and add papers the model missed. Votes show up for everyone within seconds, and the list re-orders itself. The code and the data are in the public repository.

Where the papers come from

  1. Journals (through OpenAlex): Nature, Nature Genetics, Nature Communications, Nature Reviews Genetics, Nature Medicine, Nature Human Behaviour, Science, Science Translational Medicine, Cell, Cell Genomics, American Journal of Human Genetics.
  2. Preprints: bioRxiv genetics and genomics; medRxiv genetic and genomic medicine and health informatics.
  3. Corrections, editorials and news items are dropped. A preprint whose published version appears in the same month is dropped in favor of the published one, and a paper listed in an earlier month is not listed again. The journal version of an earlier preprint can be.

The list is built on the 3rd of each month for the month before. The sources are a short configuration file, config/sources.toml.

How papers are scored

  1. Title and abstract are turned into a 512-number vector by a static text-embedding model (potion-base-32M), which needs no GPU and reads about 1,600 abstracts a second.
  2. A logistic regression on that vector, plus one indicator per journal or preprint category, gives the score. Its weights (model/weights.json) were fitted to twelve months of picks from a statistical genetics group's reading list: the papers the group chose are the positives, every other candidate a negative.
  3. The whole pool is ranked and the top 25 are shown.
  4. Learning from votes. Before each monthly run, all votes are read. Once 30 listed papers have an upvote, a correction to the weights is fitted: in every month that got votes, the listed papers with an upvote count as positives and the other listed papers as negatives. The original score is the starting point, and a penalty keeps the correction small while votes are few. Votes that agree with the score change little; surprising votes change more.

How well it works

The scorer was tested on past months of the reading list: to score a month, the model saw only earlier months, and a paper counted as found if it was among the top 25. The test covers 8 months (March to October 2026), 210 picks, and about 2,100 candidates a month. AUC is the chance that a picked paper outranks one that was not picked.

What these numbers do and do not show:

  1. The embedding scorer is close to the language model but below it: 38 against 43 picks in the top 25, and a lower AUC in two of three months. It costs nothing to run.
  2. It finds fewer than half of the picks. Tastes differ from month to month, and about one pick in ten comes from a source that is not scanned at all. Voting and adding papers are there to fill that gap.
  3. Venue carries about half the signal: the same model on text alone finds half as many picks.
  4. Scoring by similarity to past picks is clearly worse than a classifier.
  5. A single learned coefficient on each venue's past pick rate was worse than one indicator per venue (57 against 78 picks over March to September), and a three times stronger indicator made no real difference (75).
  6. Weights fitted once, on data up to May 2026, did as well on June to October as weights refitted every month (58 against 59 of 132 picks). So the site runs on fixed weights and the vote correction.
  7. Ranking the full pool finds as many picks as ranking a keyword-filtered pool (90 and 91), so there is no keyword filter.
  8. Other classifiers on the same embeddings did not beat logistic regression consistently (table below): the best on one embedding model is mid-table on the other.
  9. Larger embedding models (PubMedBERT, MedCPT, SPECTER2, SciNCL, BGE, MPNet, E5, GTE-ModernBERT) have not been compared yet.
  10. The weights in use were fitted on all twelve months up to October 2026. Lists for those months are therefore scored by a model that has seen their answers, and look better than a future month will.

Voting and adding papers

  1. There are no accounts. Your browser gets a random anonymous id, which gives it one upvote per paper; you can take the upvote back. There are no downvotes. Clearing site data or using another browser gives a new id.
  2. A name is optional. If you enter one, it is stored with your votes and the papers you add, and is visible to everyone.
  3. "Add a paper" takes a journal, a title and a URL. The short name (Journal_FirstAuthor_Year_Keyword) is generated; the first author and year are looked up at Crossref from the DOI in the URL. Adding a paper upvotes it. You can remove a paper you added.
  4. "Copy table" copies the list as shown, as four tab-separated columns (Journal, Short Name, Title, URL) that paste into a spreadsheet.
  5. Votes are stored in Google Firebase and copied into the repository each month, without names and with the ids hashed.
