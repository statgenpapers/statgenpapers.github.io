StatGen Papers

About StatGen Papers

Each month, StatGen Papers lists the statistical genetics papers that a model predicts readers will mark: every paper with a Score of at least 25%. The model chooses them from about 2,000 candidates in journals such as Nature Genetics, the American Journal of Human Genetics and Cell Genomics, and in preprints. Anyone can add papers the model missed: they join the next list, scored like the others, with a person icon beside the Score. The code is in the public repository.

Where do the papers come from?

  1. Journals (through OpenAlex): Nature, Nature Genetics, Nature Communications, Nature Reviews Genetics, Nature Medicine, Nature Human Behaviour, Science, Science Translational Medicine, Cell, Cell Genomics, American Journal of Human Genetics. Corrections, editorials and news items are left out.
  2. Preprints: bioRxiv genetics and genomics; medRxiv genetic and genomic medicine and health informatics. A preprint whose published version appears in the same month is left out in favor of the published one.
  3. A paper listed in an earlier month is not listed again; the journal version of an earlier preprint can be.

Each month's list is built on the 10th of the following month.

How does the score work?

  1. The title and abstract become a vector of 512 numbers, from a small text-embedding model (potion-base-32M) that runs on an ordinary CPU.
  2. A logistic regression on that vector and the journal or preprint category gives a raw score. It is fitted again before each list, on the candidates of the 12 months before it: papers marked on the site are the positives, all other candidates the negatives, and recent months count more (a month's weight halves every 6 months), so it follows tastes as they change. The first marks were made before the site opened.
  3. The raw score is calibrated into a probability, the Score. Every paper at 25% or more is listed, so a month with more promising papers has a longer list.
  4. Marks re-order the list at once, and the next month's model learns from them.

What is the score?

The model's prediction of how likely a paper is to be marked. In the test below, papers at about 50% were marked about half the time. Marks re-order the list but do not change the score.

Does the score work?

Each of 8 past months (March to October 2026) was scored by a model trained only on the months before it.

  1. A marked paper outranks an unmarked one 96% of the time (AUC), and the top 25 hold 90 of the 210 marked papers: fewer than half. Tastes differ from month to month, and about one marked paper in ten comes from a source that is not scanned. Adding papers fills that gap.
  2. It comes close to a language model (Claude Haiku) that read and graded each paper: on the same papers in August to October, 38 against 43 marked papers in the top 25, at no cost.
  3. From 2026/01 to 2026/10, the 25% cutoff listed 10 to 28 papers a month and caught 111 of the 239 marked papers, as many as a fixed 25 a month, with fewer papers.

Why do the months before 2026/11 already have marks?

The lists for 2026/01 to 2026/10 were made as if the site had run then: each month by a model trained only on the months before it. Their marks, and the papers added to them, were made before the site opened. From 2026/11 the lists are made live.

How do I give feedback?

Open an issue on GitHub: a bug, a journal or preprint category to add, or anything else.
